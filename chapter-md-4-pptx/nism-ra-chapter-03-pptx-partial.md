# 3.2 Terminology in Debt Market

## Slide 1: Coupon and Maturity
*   **Coupon Rate:** The fixed annual interest rate paid on a bond's face value.
*   **Annual Interest Formula:** 
$$\text{Interest Amount} = \text{Face Value} \times \text{Coupon Rate}$$
*   **Tenor:** Maturity is the single largest factor in bond price changes.

### Notes
Every debt security is defined by how much is borrowed, for how long, and at what rate. The coupon rate is the annual interest rate expressed as a percentage of the face value. For an eight point two four percent coupon bond with a face value of one thousand rupees, the annual interest is eighty-two rupees and forty paise. Maturity, or tenor, is the date the principal must be repaid.

***

## Slide 2: Holding Period Return (HPR)
*   **Total Return:** Income earned during a specific period of ownership.
*   **HPR Formula:** 
$$\text{HPR} = \frac{\text{Coupon} + \text{Reinvestment Income} + (\text{Sale Price} - \text{Purchase Price})}{\text{Purchase Price}}$$
*   **Limitation:** A crude measure that ignores the effect of compounding.

### Notes
The Holding Period Return measures the total excess amount generated over the initial investment. To calculate it, add the coupon payments received, any interest earned from reinvesting those coupons, and the capital gain or loss from selling the bond. Divide this sum by the purchase price to get the percentage return. For example, a bond bought at one hundred and four rupees that earns eight rupees in coupons and six rupees in capital gains provides a fourteen percent holding period return.

***

## Slide 3: Current Yield and Yield to Maturity (YTM)
*   **Current Yield Formula:** 
$$\text{Current Yield} = \frac{\text{Annual Coupon}}{\text{Current Market Price}}$$
*   **YTM Definition:** The comprehensive Internal Rate of Return (IRR) if held to maturity.
*   **YTM Logic:** Equates the bond's price with the present value of all future inflows.

### Notes
Yield measures help compare bond returns. Current yield is a simple calculation where you divide the annual interest payment by the current market price. Yield to Maturity, or Y-T-M, is more accurate as it considers all future coupons, reinvestment income, and the final redemption value. It is the interest rate that makes the present value of all these future cash flows equal to the bond's current market price.

***

## Slide 4: Duration and Price Sensitivity
*   **Modified Duration Formula:** 
$$\text{Modified Duration} = \frac{\text{Macaulay's Duration}}{1 + \text{yield}}$$
*   **Risk Metric:** Measures the percentage change in price for a 1% change in interest rates.
*   **Relationship:** Higher maturity and lower coupons lead to higher duration.

### Notes
Duration is a measure of interest rate risk. Modified Duration specifically tells you how much a bond's price will move for every one percent change in market interest rates. It is calculated by taking the Macaulay duration—which is the weighted average time to receive your investment back—and dividing it by the sum of one plus the yield. Bonds with higher modified duration are more sensitive and riskier when interest rates fluctuate.

# 3.3 Commodity Market Terminology

## Slide 1: Basis and Market Contango/Backwardation
*   **Basis Formula:** 
$$\text{Basis} = \text{Spot Price} - \text{Futures Price}$$
*   **Contango:** 
$$\text{Futures Price} > \text{Spot Price}$$
*   **Backwardation:** 
$$\text{Futures Price} < \text{Spot Price}$$

### Notes
In the commodity market, the Basis is the difference between the current cash price, called the spot price, and the price of a futures contract. If the futures price is higher than the spot price, the market is in Contango, usually indicating expectations of rising prices or high storage costs. If the futures price is lower than the spot price, it is in Backwardation, suggesting the market expects prices to fall.

***

## Slide 2: Fair Value of Futures (Cost of Carry)
*   **Cost of Carry:** Storage, insurance, transport, and financing costs.
*   **Fair Value Formula:** 
$$\text{Fair Value} = \text{Spot Price} + [\text{Spot Price} \times \text{Rate} \times (\frac{\text{Time}}{12})]$$

### Notes
The fair value of a commodity futures contract is determined by adding the "cost of carry" to the current spot price. The cost of carry includes all the expenses involved in holding a physical commodity—such as storage, insurance, and interest—until the future delivery date. For example, if gold is one lakh two thousand rupees and the annual carry cost is eight percent, the three-month fair value is calculated by adding two thousand and forty rupees in carry costs to the spot price.
