## Exchange Traded Funds (ETFs)

Exchange Traded Funds (ETFs) combine some of the best features of both **mutual funds** and **equity shares**. Like a mutual fund, an ETF holds a diversified portfolio of assets. Like a share, it is traded on a stock exchange throughout the trading day.

ETFs were introduced to provide investors with a low-cost, transparent, and convenient way to gain exposure to an entire market index, commodity, sector, or asset class through a single security.

Today, ETFs are among the fastest-growing investment products globally and have become increasingly popular in India.

### Issuer – Mutual Funds

ETFs are launched by Asset Management Companies (AMCs), just like mutual fund schemes.

Examples:

* SBI Mutual Fund
* Nippon India Mutual Fund
* HDFC Mutual Fund
* ICICI Prudential Mutual Fund
* UTI Mutual Fund

Each ETF is structured as a mutual fund scheme but trades differently from ordinary mutual funds.

### Investors

ETFs are suitable for both:

#### Individual Investors

* Retail investors
* Salaried employees
* HNIs
* Long-term investors

#### Institutional Investors

* Mutual Funds
* Insurance Companies
* Pension Funds
* Banks
* FPIs

Institutional investors often use ETFs for tactical asset allocation and liquidity management.

### Medium

Unlike traditional mutual funds, ETF units are:

* Created through Authorized Participants (APs)
* Listed on stock exchanges
* Bought and sold during market hours

Thus ETFs involve both:

* Primary Market transactions (creation/redemption)
* Secondary Market transactions (exchange trading)

### Regulator

ETFs in India are regulated by **SEBI** under mutual fund regulations.

SEBI regulates:

* ETF launches
* Portfolio disclosures
* Investor protection
* Tracking standards
* Valuation practices

## Definition

* Investment vehicle that invests pooled money to track an index, commodity (e.g., Gold), or basket of assets.

## How ETFs Work

An ETF collects money from investors and invests it in a portfolio designed to replicate a specific benchmark.

Examples:

| ETF Type   | Benchmark            |
| ---------- | -------------------- |
| Nifty ETF  | Nifty 50             |
| Sensex ETF | Sensex               |
| Bank ETF   | Nifty Bank           |
| Gold ETF   | Physical Gold        |
| PSU ETF    | Basket of PSU Stocks |

The objective is not to outperform the benchmark but to closely replicate its performance.


## Key Features

* Like an index fund (portfolio reflects index it tracks)
* **Unlike index fund**: Units are listed and traded on stock exchange
* Price changes continuously (like regular shares)


## Understanding These Features

### Like an Index Fund

Most ETFs are passive investment vehicles.

For example:

A Nifty 50 ETF will invest in the same 50 stocks that constitute the Nifty 50 Index and in approximately the same proportion.

If Nifty rises 10%:

* ETF should also rise approximately 10%.

The objective is to mirror the index, not beat it.

### Listed on Stock Exchanges

Unlike ordinary mutual funds, ETF units are traded on exchanges such as:

* NSE
* BSE

Investors require:

* Trading Account
* Demat Account

to buy and sell ETFs.

This makes ETFs behave more like shares than traditional mutual funds.

### Continuous Price Discovery

Traditional mutual funds are bought or redeemed at the day's NAV.

ETF prices, however:

* Change every second during trading hours.
* Depend on market demand and supply.

Therefore ETF investors can immediately react to market movements.

## Creation Process

1. Sponsor (MF or global asset manager)
2. Authorised Participants (large broking houses, international banks) create bundle of assets
3. Authorised Participants deliver bundle to sponsor
4. Sponsor issues ETF units
5. ETF units sold to retail investors and traded like regular shares

## Understanding the Creation Process

The ETF creation mechanism is one of the most unique aspects of ETFs.

### Sponsor

The sponsor is usually:

* Mutual Fund House
* Asset Management Company
* Global Asset Manager

Examples:

* SBI Mutual Fund
* Nippon India Mutual Fund
* BlackRock (globally)

The sponsor designs and launches the ETF.

### Authorised Participants (APs)

Authorized Participants are large financial institutions.

Examples:

* Large Banks
* Investment Banks
* Major Brokerage Houses

They play a crucial role in ensuring ETF liquidity.

### Creation of ETF Units

Suppose an ETF tracks Nifty 50.

The AP purchases:

* The underlying basket of Nifty stocks

and transfers that basket to the ETF sponsor.

In return, the sponsor issues ETF units.

This process is known as **Creation**.

### Redemption of ETF Units

The reverse process is called **Redemption**.

The AP returns ETF units to the sponsor and receives the underlying basket of securities.

This mechanism helps keep ETF prices close to the value of underlying assets.

## Why ETF Prices Usually Stay Close to NAV

One important concept for exams is **arbitrage**.

### Example

Suppose:

ETF NAV = ₹100

Market Price = ₹105

APs can:

* Create new ETF units at NAV
* Sell them in market at ₹105

This arbitrage profit encourages supply and pushes the ETF price back toward NAV.

Similarly, if ETF trades below NAV, APs can buy ETF units and redeem them.

Thus ETF prices usually remain close to intrinsic value.

## Types of ETFs

Although the official note does not classify ETFs, investors commonly encounter the following categories.

### Equity ETFs

Track stock market indices.

Examples:

* Nifty 50 ETF
* Sensex ETF
* Nifty Next 50 ETF

### Gold ETFs

Invest primarily in physical gold.

Advantages:

* No storage concerns
* No purity concerns
* Easy liquidity

Gold ETFs have become a popular alternative to buying physical gold.

### Debt ETFs

Track bond portfolios or debt indices.

Examples:

* Bharat Bond ETF

These are suitable for investors seeking relatively stable returns.

### Sector ETFs

Track specific sectors.

Examples:

* Banking ETF
* IT ETF
* PSU ETF

Investors use these when they have a positive outlook on a particular sector.

### International ETFs

Provide exposure to foreign markets.

Examples:

* US market ETFs
* Global technology ETFs

They allow geographical diversification.

## Advantages

* Diversification benefits of index fund
* Can buy/sell even one unit at real-time prices
* Passively managed → lower expense ratios than mutual funds

## Understanding the Advantages

### Diversification

A single ETF unit can provide exposure to dozens or hundreds of securities.

Example:

Buying one Nifty ETF indirectly gives exposure to 50 major companies.

### Liquidity

Investors can:

* Buy during market hours
* Sell during market hours

No need to wait for end-of-day NAV.

### Lower Costs

Since ETFs are usually passively managed:

* Research costs are lower
* Fund management costs are lower

Therefore expense ratios are typically lower than actively managed mutual funds.

### Transparency

ETF portfolios are generally disclosed regularly.

Investors know exactly what they own.

### Simplicity

Instead of selecting individual stocks, investors can buy a single ETF to obtain diversified market exposure.

## ETFs vs Index Funds

This distinction is frequently tested.

| Aspect        | ETF                          | Index Fund              |
| ------------- | ---------------------------- | ----------------------- |
| Trading       | On stock exchange            | Directly with fund      |
| Demat Account | Required                     | Not required            |
| Price         | Changes continuously         | NAV-based               |
| Purchase Time | Any time during market hours | End-of-day NAV          |
| Liquidity     | Through exchange trading     | Through fund house      |
| Expense Ratio | Usually lower                | Usually slightly higher |


## ETFs vs Mutual Funds

| Aspect            | ETF             | Traditional Mutual Fund    |
| ----------------- | --------------- | -------------------------- |
| Management Style  | Mostly passive  | Active or passive          |
| Trading           | Exchange traded | Direct purchase/redemption |
| Real-Time Pricing | Yes             | No                         |
| Expense Ratio     | Lower           | Higher                     |
| Demat Requirement | Usually yes     | Usually no                 |

## Research Analyst's Perspective

Research Analysts closely monitor ETFs because:

* ETF flows influence stock demand.
* Large ETF purchases can move market prices.
* Passive investing is becoming increasingly important.
* ETF inflows often indicate investor sentiment toward specific sectors or markets.

Today, passive investing through ETFs has become a major force influencing capital flows in global and Indian markets.

---

### Key Takeaway

An Exchange Traded Fund (ETF) is a pooled investment vehicle that tracks an index, commodity, sector, or basket of assets while trading on a stock exchange like an ordinary share. ETFs combine the diversification benefits of mutual funds with the liquidity and real-time pricing of stocks. Their passive management structure typically results in lower costs, making them one of the most efficient investment vehicles available to modern investors.
