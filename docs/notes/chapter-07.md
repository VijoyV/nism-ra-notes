# Chapter 7: Company Analysis – Business and Governance

Company analysis is the final, granular level of the **E-I-C (Economy-Industry-Company) framework**. While macro and industry factors set the stage, company-specific factors determine which individual business will outperform its peers.

---

## 7.1 Role of Company Analysis in Fundamental Research
Analysts focus on internal drivers such as business models, management quality, and competitive advantages to assess if a company can exploit industry opportunities and withstand external threats. This qualitative study provides the context needed before diving into the numerical financial analysis.

---

## 7.2 Understanding Business and Business Models
Analysts must start with a fundamental question: **"Do I understand the business?"**.
- **Core Logic:** Research should prioritize firms whose products, customers, and delivery mechanisms are clear.
- **Sector Specificity:** Each industry has unique KPIs. For example, **Footfalls** are critical for Retail, **Net Interest Margin (NIM)** for Banking, and **Average Revenue Per User (ARPU)** for Telecom.
- **Model vs. Product:** Competition is often between business models (how value is created/delivered) rather than just the products themselves.

---

## 7.3 Pricing Power and Sustainability
**Pricing Power** is the ability to independently determine prices without losing customers to competitors.
- **Drivers:** It is influenced by industry factors (competition intensity) and company-specific factors like **brand loyalty** and a **low-cost base**.
- **Impact:** Companies with high pricing power can pass on raw material cost escalations to customers, protecting their profit margins.

---

## 7.4 Competitive Advantages and Differentiation
To outperform, a company must distinguish itself in three primary areas:

| Area of Differentiation | Focus | Key Driver |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Product Differentiation** | Unique features, quality, or functionality. | Strong R&D and a culture of innovation. |
| **Competitive Pricing** | Offering better value for money than peers. | Operational efficiency and a low-cost advantage. |
| **Execution** | Better branding, sales strategy, and positioning. | Management track record and sales force focus. |

---

## 7.5 SWOT Analysis Framework
SWOT analysis evaluates internal capabilities versus external dynamics.

- **Strengths (Internal):** Financial strength, patents (IP), low customer concentration, and strong track record.
- **Weaknesses (Internal):** High fixed costs, weak margins, high debt, or lack of experience in new segments.
- **Opportunities (External):** Technological shifts, geographical expansion, or industry consolidation.
- **Threats (External):** Economic recession, regulatory changes, or disruptive new technologies.

---

## 7.6 Quality of Management and Governance
Because ownership is separate from management, **Agency Risk** exists—the risk that managers may not act in the best interest of owners.

### I. Evaluating Management Competency
Analysts assess "C-level" executives based on:
- **Experience & Track Record:** Past performance in similar roles.
- **Vision:** Long-term strategic direction and goal-setting.
- **Execution:** History of meeting or exceeding stated guidance.
- **Succession Planning:** Plans for continuity if top management leaves.

### II. Corporate Governance Standards
These are the rules and processes that ensure a company is run ethically. Key indicators include:
- **Board Composition:** At least 1/3 to 1/2 of the board should be **Independent Directors**.
- **Role Separation:** The Chairman of the Board should ideally not be the CEO/MD (mandated for the top 1,000 listed companies).
- **Auditor Integrity:** Auditors should be rotated every 5 years and not be overly dependent on the fees from a single group.
- **Related Party Transactions:** Transactions with promoter-owned entities must be at "arms-length" and reviewed by the audit committee.

---

## 7.7 Business Risks
Analysts must constantly ask: **"What could go wrong?"**. A good management team is transparent about risks—such as currency fluctuations or execution delays—and has mitigation strategies in place.

---

## 7.8 Credit Rating History
A company's **Credit Rating** indicates its ability to service debt. Tracking changes in these ratings over time reveals financial risk levels and how responsive management is to the concerns raised by rating agencies.

---

## 7.9 ESG Framework (Environment, Social, and Governance)
Modern investors use ESG filters to identify sustainable businesses.
- **Environmental:** Carbon footprint, pollution control, and resource efficiency.
- **Social:** Human rights, gender equality, and community relations.
- **Governance:** Ethics and shareholder rights.
- **Regulatory Note:** SEBI now mandates top 1,000 companies to provide ESG disclosures via the **Business Responsibility and Sustainability Report (BRSR)**.

---

### **Quick Facts for Revision**
- **Moat:** A term for high entry barriers that protect a company's "economic castle".
- **Promoter Pledging:** Promoters may take loans against their shares; high pledging can lead to sharp price drops if lenders are forced to sell the collateral.
- **BRSR:** The official framework for ESG reporting in India starting FY2023.
- **Arms-Length:** A principle requiring transactions between related parties to be conducted as if they were between independent entities.
- **Keyman Risk:** The risk to an organization if its leadership is too concentrated in a few individuals without a succession plan.
