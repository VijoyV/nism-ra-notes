## Participants of the Financial Market

The financial market functions as an ecosystem where funds move from those who have surplus money to those who need money. This ecosystem operates efficiently because of the interaction among four key participants:

| Participant                        | Role                                                                    |
| ---------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Investors / Providers of Funds** | Supply capital by purchasing securities                                 |
| **Borrowers / Seekers of Funds**   | Raise capital by issuing securities                                     |
| **Intermediaries**                 | Facilitate issuance, trading, settlement, and safekeeping of securities |
| **Regulatory Bodies**              | Ensure fairness, transparency, stability, and investor protection       |

---

## 1. Investors / Providers of Funds

Investors are the source of capital in the financial system. They invest their savings with the expectation of earning returns through dividends, interest, capital appreciation, or a combination of these.

### Types of Investors

### 1.1 Retail Investors

These are individual investors investing their personal savings.

Examples:

* Salaried employees
* Business owners
* Professionals
* Retired individuals

Typical investments:

* Shares
* Mutual Funds
* Bonds
* ETFs
* Government Securities

---

### 1.2 Institutional Investors

These organizations manage large pools of money.

Examples:

#### Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs)

* Life Insurance Corporation of India (LIC)
* Mutual Funds
* Pension Funds
* Insurance Companies

Examples of Mutual Fund Houses:

* SBI Mutual Fund
* HDFC Mutual Fund
* ICICI Prudential Mutual Fund

---

### 1.3 Foreign Portfolio Investors (FPIs)

Foreign investors investing in Indian securities.

Examples:

* Global pension funds
* Sovereign wealth funds
* Foreign mutual funds
* Hedge funds

Examples:

* BlackRock
* Vanguard Group
* Government Pension Fund Global

### Why Investors Matter

Without investors:

* Companies cannot raise capital.
* Governments cannot borrow efficiently.
* Economic growth slows down.

Investors are therefore the **suppliers of capital** to the economy.

---

## 2. Borrowers / Seekers of Funds

Borrowers are entities that require money for expansion, operations, infrastructure development, or government spending.

### 2.1 Corporates

Companies raise funds through:

#### Equity

By issuing:

* IPOs
* FPOs
* Rights Issues

Examples:

* Reliance Industries
* Tata Consultancy Services
* Infosys

---

### 2.2 Government

Governments borrow for:

* Infrastructure projects
* Welfare schemes
* Budget deficits

Through:

* Treasury Bills (T-Bills)
* Government Securities (G-Secs)
* State Development Loans (SDLs)

Examples:

* Government of India
* State Governments

---

### 2.3 Financial Institutions

Banks and financial institutions also raise funds through debt instruments.

Examples:

* State Bank of India
* HDFC Bank
* Power Finance Corporation

### Why Borrowers Matter

Borrowers use the capital raised to:

* Build factories
* Expand businesses
* Create jobs
* Develop infrastructure

Thus, they convert savings into productive economic activity.

---

## 3. Intermediaries

Intermediaries provide the infrastructure that makes the securities market function smoothly.

Without intermediaries, investors and issuers would struggle to find each other, trade securely, or settle transactions.

---

### 3.1 Stock Exchanges

Stock exchanges provide a regulated platform where securities are bought and sold.

Major Indian Stock Exchanges:

#### National Stock Exchange of India (NSE)

* Largest stock exchange in India
* Benchmark Index: Nifty 50

#### BSE Limited (BSE)

* Asia's oldest stock exchange
* Benchmark Index: Sensex

Functions:

* Trading platform
* Price discovery
* Market surveillance
* Transparency

---

### 3.2 Stock Brokers

Brokers act as intermediaries between investors and stock exchanges.

Examples:

* Zerodha
* Groww
* Angel One
* ICICI Direct

Functions:

* Trade execution
* Research
* Advisory
* Trading platforms

---

### 3.3 Depositories

Depositories hold securities in electronic (demat) form.

India has two depositories:

#### National Securities Depository Limited (NSDL)

Established in 1996.

#### Central Depository Services Limited (CDSL)

Established in 1999.

Functions:

* Hold securities electronically
* Facilitate transfer of ownership
* Enable dematerialization

Think of a depository as a **bank for securities**.

---

### 3.4 Depository Participants (DPs)

Investors cannot directly open accounts with NSDL/CDSL.

Instead they open demat accounts through DPs.

Examples:

* Banks
* Brokers
* Financial institutions

Examples:

* SBI Securities
* HDFC Securities
* Zerodha

---

### 3.5 Clearing Corporations

Clearing corporations ensure that every trade is settled successfully.

Major clearing corporations:

#### National Securities Clearing Corporation Limited (NSCCL)

Associated with NSE.

#### Indian Clearing Corporation Limited (ICCL)

Associated with BSE.

Functions:

* Guarantee settlement
* Manage counterparty risk
* Collect margins
* Ensure delivery of securities and funds

---

### 3.6 Merchant Bankers

Merchant bankers help companies raise capital.

Functions:

* IPO management
* Due diligence
* Prospectus preparation
* Regulatory compliance

Examples:

* ICICI Securities
* Axis Capital
* Kotak Mahindra Capital Company

---

### 3.7 Credit Rating Agencies

These agencies assess the creditworthiness of debt issuers.

Examples:

* CRISIL
* ICRA
* CARE Ratings

---

## 4. Regulatory Bodies

Regulators maintain market integrity and protect investors.

---

### 4.1 SEBI (Securities and Exchange Board of India)

#### Securities and Exchange Board of India

SEBI is the primary regulator of the Indian securities market.

Functions:

* Protect investors
* Regulate intermediaries
* Prevent market manipulation
* Approve public issues
* Develop securities markets

For a Research Analyst, SEBI is the most important regulator.

---

### 4.2 RBI (Reserve Bank of India)

#### Reserve Bank of India

Functions:

* Monetary policy
* Banking regulation
* Government debt management
* Currency management

---

### 4.3 Ministry of Finance

#### Ministry of Finance

Functions:

* Financial legislation
* Fiscal policy
* Capital market policy oversight

---

### 4.4 IRDAI

#### Insurance Regulatory and Development Authority of India

Regulates:

* Insurance companies
* Insurance products

---

### 4.5 PFRDA

#### Pension Fund Regulatory and Development Authority

Regulates:

* Pension funds
* National Pension System (NPS)

---

## How All Participants Work Together (Exam Perspective)

A useful flow to remember is:

**Investors → Brokers → Stock Exchange → Clearing Corporation → Depository → Borrowers/Issuers**

For example:

1. A company such as Reliance needs ₹10,000 crore.
2. It issues securities through the market.
3. Merchant bankers manage the issue.
4. Investors subscribe through brokers.
5. Shares are credited to demat accounts via NSDL/CDSL.
6. NSE/BSE enable trading.
7. Clearing corporations guarantee settlement.
8. SEBI supervises the entire ecosystem.

This flow represents the complete life cycle of capital movement in the Indian securities market and is a favorite area for conceptual NISM questions.
