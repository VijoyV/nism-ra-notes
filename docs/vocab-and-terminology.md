# Key Vocabulary & Market Terminology

For a candidate from a non-financial background, Chapter 3 is arguably the most critical "vocabulary" chapter in the entire workbook. While it only accounts for 2 marks on its own, the terminology introduced here forms the essential language required to understand Chapter 8 (Financial Analysis) and Chapter 10 (Valuation Principles), which together represent 24% of the exam weightage.

Below is an elaboration on the key terms and formulas from the NISM workbook, the primary official public resource for this certification.

## 1. Equity Market Terminology

These terms help you distinguish between what a company is "worth" on paper versus what it costs to "buy" in the market.

* **Intrinsic Value vs. Market Value:** Intrinsic value is the estimated "true" value of a share based on future earning potential, while Market Value is the current trading price in the stock market.
* **Undervalued:** $\text{Intrinsic Value} > \text{Market Value}$
* **Overvalued:** $\text{Intrinsic Value} < \text{Market Value}$


* **Market Capitalization (Market Cap):** This is the total market value of the company’s equity.
* **Formula:** $\text{Market Cap} = \text{Current Market Price (CMP)} \times \text{Total Outstanding Shares}$


* **Enterprise Value (EV):** This represents the theoretical cost to buy the entire business, including its debt, while adjusting for its cash.
* **Formula (Standalone):** $\text{EV} = (\text{Market Value of Equity} + \text{Preferred Capital} + \text{Debt}) - (\text{Cash and Cash Equivalents})$



## 2. Common Valuation Ratios

For a non-financial person, these are the "shorthand" metrics used to quickly judge if a stock is cheap or expensive.

* **Price-to-Earnings (P/E) Ratio:** Measures what the market is willing to pay for every ₹1 of profit.
* **Formula:** $\text{P/E Ratio} = \frac{\text{Market Price per Share}}{\text{Earnings Per Share (EPS)}}$


* **Price-to-Book Value (P/BV) Ratio:** Compares the stock price to its "accounting value" (assets minus liabilities).
* **Formula:** $\text{P/BV Ratio} = \frac{\text{CMP}}{\text{Book Value per Share}}$


* **Price-to-Sales (P/S) Ratio:** Useful for valuing companies that aren't profitable yet but have high sales.
* **Formula:** $\text{P/S Ratio} = \frac{\text{CMP}}{\text{Annual Net Sales per Share}}$



## 3. Debt Market Terminology

Debt involves lending money. The "risk" here is usually about whether you will get your interest and principal back.

* **Coupon Rate vs. Yield:** The Coupon is the fixed interest rate promised on the Face Value (e.g., 8%), whereas the Yield is the actual return based on the price you paid.
* **Yield to Maturity (YTM):** The total interest you expect to earn if you hold the bond until it finishes (matures).
* **Duration:** This measures how sensitive a bond's price is to changes in interest rates.
* *Rule of Thumb:* If interest rates rise, bond prices fall. Higher duration means the price will fall more.



## 4. Commodity Market Terminology

* **Contango:** When the future price is higher than the current spot price (usually due to storage costs).
* **Backwardation:** When the future price is lower than the current spot price.
* **Basis:** The difference between the current price and the future price.
* **Formula:** $\text{Basis} = \text{Spot Price} - \text{Futures Price}$
