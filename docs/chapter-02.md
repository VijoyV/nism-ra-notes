## 2.1 Introduction to Securities and Securities Market

### Definition of Securities
- Transferrable financial claims or contracts.
- Evidence either:
    - Indebtedness/promise of the issuer (borrower) to the holder (investor), OR
    - Ownership interest in assets of an incorporated entity.

### Examples of Securities
- Equity shares
- Preference shares
- Debentures
- Bonds

### Issuers vs Investors
- **Issuers**: companies, financial institutions, government → raise money at a cost.
- **Investors**: those with surplus money → convert savings into financial assets that provide a return.

### Role of Secondary Market
- Allows investors to transfer rights/interests without impacting the issuer.
- A long-term security effectively becomes short-term due to active secondary market liquidity.

### What is a Securities Market?
- Brings together many buyers and sellers.
- Creates **liquidity** – ability to buy/sell at close to market prices.
- Transfers idle/surplus resources from savers to those who need funds for productive use.
- Converts savings into investments.

### Legal Definition (SCRA Section 2(h))
Securities include:
1. Shares, scrips, stocks, bonds, debentures, debenture stock
2. Derivatives
3. Units of collective investment schemes
4. Security receipts (SARFAESI Act)
5. Mutual fund units (EXCLUDES ULIPs)
6. Units of pooled investment vehicles
7. Certificates issued by SPVs against debt/receivables
8. Government securities
9. Electronic Gold Receipts (gazette notification Dec 2021)
10. Any other securities declared by Central Government
11. Rights or interests in securities

### Investors in Indian Securities Market
- Wide choice based on risk appetite and return expectations.
- Broad categories: Equity, Debt, Derivatives.

### 2.2.2 Debentures/Bonds/Notes

| Aspect | Details |
|--------|---------|
| Issuers | Companies, Government, SPVs, other entities |
| Investors | Institutional and Individual |
| Medium | Direct issuance through Stock Exchange (if listed) |
| Regulators | RBI, SEBI, MCA, NCLT |

#### Types of Debentures
| Type | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| **Fully Convertible (FCD)** | Fully converted into ordinary shares; terms specified at issue |
| **Partly Convertible (PCD)** | Partly converted; non-convertible part redeemed normally |
| **Non-Convertible (NCD)** | Pure debt; no conversion; repayable on maturity |

#### Short-Term Debt Instruments (≤ 1 year)
- **Treasury Bills** – issued by government
- **Commercial Papers** – issued by high-credit-rating companies
- **Certificate of Deposit** – issued by banks

#### Domestic vs External Bonds
| Type | Where issued | Currency |
|------|-------------|----------|
| Domestic Bonds | Home country | Home currency |
| External Bonds | Foreign/offshore markets | Different from home currency |

**External Bonds subclassification:**
- **Foreign Bond**: issued in foreign country, denominated in that country's local currency
- **Euro Bond**: issued in foreign country, denominated in a currency NOT local to that country

### 2.2.2.1 Foreign bonds (Detailed)

| Aspect | Details |
|--------|---------|
| Issuers | Companies, Government, SPVs |
| Investors | Institutional and Individual |
| Medium | Direct issuance through Stock Exchanges |
| Regulator | Regulators in respective country of issue |

**Key Features:**
- Issued by a company in a currency different from its home currency.
- Denominated in the currency of the country where it is issued.
- Example: Delhi International Airport Limited (GMR SPV) issued USD bonds in US.

**Risk for Issuer:**
- Creates significant foreign currency risk.
- If foreign currency appreciates against domestic currency, issuer needs more domestic currency to repay.

**Why companies do it:**
- USD or other mature economy currencies carry significantly lower interest rates than emerging markets.

---

### 2.2.2.2 Euro bonds / External Bonds (Detailed)

| Aspect | Details |
|--------|---------|
| Issuers | Companies, Government, SPVs |
| Investors | Institutional and Individual |
| Medium | Direct issuance through Stock Exchanges |
| Regulator | Regulators in respective country of issue |

**Key Features:**
- Issued in a currency different from the currency of the country in which it is issued.
- Example: US dollar denominated bonds issued in Kuwait (currency = Kuwaiti Dinar).

**Masala Bonds (Special Category):**
- Euro bonds denominated in Indian Rupees (INR)
- Issued outside India
- First issued: November 2014 by International Finance Corporation
- Listed on London Stock Exchange

**Key Difference from Foreign Bonds:**
| | Foreign Bonds | Masala Bonds (Euro Bonds) |
|--|---------------|---------------------------|
| Currency risk bearer | Issuer | Investor |
| Reason | If foreign currency appreciates | If INR depreciates against investor's local currency |

**Definition:**
- Tracks market movement using prices of a specific number of shares chosen as representative sample.
- Most leading indices are weighted by **market capitalisation**.

**Most widely tracked indices in India:**
| Index | Exchange | Number of Stocks |
|-------|----------|------------------|

- S&P BSE 100, BSE 500, BSE MidCap, BSE SmallCap

**Major Uses of Indices:**
1. Reference point to compare returns of other asset classes (gold, debt)

3. Barometer of the economy or a sector
4. Real-time indicator of market sentiments
5. Underlying for Index Funds, Index Futures and Options

### 2.2.5 Mutual Fund Units

| Aspect | Details |
|--------|---------|
| Issuer | Mutual Funds |
| Investors | Institutional and Individual |
| Medium | Direct issuance by mutual funds |
| Regulator | SEBI |

**Definition:**
- Investment vehicle that pools money from many investors.
- Invests the pool in a portfolio of assets reflecting a common theme/objective.
- Each investor's share = **unit**.

**Net Asset Value (NAV):**
- Value of each unit.
- Changes continuously reflecting changes in portfolio value.

**Types:**
| Type | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| **Open-ended** | Buy from fund anytime; sell back to fund anytime; no fixed maturity |
| **Close-ended** | Fixed maturity period; limited liquidity |

---

### 2.2.6 Exchange Traded Funds (ETFs)

| Aspect | Details |
|--------|---------|
| Issuer | Mutual Funds |
| Investors | Institutional and Individual |
| Medium | Direct issuance + marketed by Authorised Participants |
| Regulator | SEBI |

**Definition:**
- Investment vehicle that invests pooled money to track an index, commodity (e.g., Gold), or basket of assets.

**Key Features:**
- Like an index fund (portfolio reflects index it tracks)
- **Unlike index fund**: Units are listed and traded on stock exchange
- Price changes continuously (like regular shares)

**Creation Process:**
1. Sponsor (MF or global asset manager)
2. Authorised Participants (large broking houses, international banks) create bundle of assets
3. Authorised Participants deliver bundle to sponsor
4. Sponsor issues ETF units
5. ETF units sold to retail investors and traded like regular shares

**Advantages:**
- Diversification benefits of index fund
- Can buy/sell even one unit at real-time prices
- Passively managed → lower expense ratios than mutual funds

### 2.2.7 Hybrids/Structured Products

#### 2.2.7.1 Preference Shares

**Definition:**
- Special kind of equity shares with **preference over common/ordinary equity shares** at:
    - Time of dividend
    - Time of repayment of capital in winding up

**Dual Nature:**
| Like Equity | Like Debt |
|-------------|-----------|
| Called shareholders (not creditors) | Pre-determined rate of dividend |
| Payment called "dividend" | Dividend payable before common equity |
| Paid from Profit After Tax (PAT) | Preference in winding up (after debt, before equity) |
| Not an obligation unlike interest | |

**What they DO NOT have:**
- Voting rights
- Right over residual assets in winding up

**Types:**
- Cumulative (unpaid dividend carried forward)
- Non-cumulative (unpaid dividend lapses)
- Convertible (partly or fully)

---

#### 2.2.7.2 Convertible Debentures & Bonds

**Definition:**
- Debt instruments that can be converted into equity shares at a future date.
- Have features of both debt and equity.

**Types:**

| Type | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| **Fully Convertible (FCD)** | Entire face value converted into equity |
| **Partly Convertible (PCD)** | Portion converted to equity; non-convertible portion remains debt, earns interest, repaid on redemption |
| **Optionally Convertible (OCD)** | Convertible at discretion of debenture holder |

**Conversion Details Specified at Issue:**
- Date of conversion
- Conversion ratio (number of shares per debenture)
- Conversion price (usually at discount to market price)
- Proportion to be converted (for PCD)

**Advantage to Issuer:**
- Lower coupon rate than pure debt (investor gets potential capital appreciation)
- No repayment on maturity (shares issued instead)

**Disadvantage to Issuer:**
- Dilution of existing equity shareholders' stake

**Advantage to Investor:**
- Earn coupon income in initial stage (when project is nascent)
- Benefit from share appreciation after conversion

---

#### 2.2.7.3 Indian Depository Receipts (IDRs), Global Depository Receipts (GDRs) and American Depository Receipts (ADRs)

**Definition:**
- Financial instruments that represent shares of a foreign company.
- Traded in a market of a country where they are issued.
- Denominated in local currency of that country.

**Issuance Process:**
1. Company or investor delivers equity shares to a depository (foreign bank) in the target market.
2. Depository places shares in custodian account in company's home country.
3. Depository issues a certificate (Depositary Receipt) against those shares to overseas investors.

**Sponsored vs Unsponsored:**
| Type | Initiated by | Listing | Regulatory requirements |
|------|-------------|---------|------------------------|
| Sponsored DR | Issuing company | Can be listed on exchanges | Must comply with listing requirements |
| Unsponsored DR | Investor | OTC markets only | Less regulation |

**Two-way Fungibility:**
- Subject to regulatory provisions.
- Shares bought in home country → converted to DRs in foreign market.
- DRs bought in foreign market → converted to underlying shares in home country.

**Country-specific DRs:**
| Type | Market |
|------|--------|
| ADR | USA |
| IDR | India |
| HKDR | Hong Kong |
| GDR | Multiple countries (typically EU) |

**Indian Context:**
- Indian companies can raise equity capital abroad via DRs.
- Foreign companies can raise equity capital in India via IDRs (SEBI guidelines: lock-in, only resident Indian investors, etc.).

---

#### 2.2.7.4 Foreign Currency Convertible Bonds (FCCBs)

**Definition:**
- Foreign currency (usually USD) denominated convertible debt securities issued by companies in international markets.

**Key Features:**
- Like convertibles, but generally optionally convertible.
- Issued offshore in different denominations.
- Regulated by RBI under FEMA (Issue of Foreign Currency Convertible Bonds and Ordinary Shares through Depository Receipt Mechanism, 1993).

**Cash Flows:**
- Interest and principal repayment (if happens) → in foreign currency.
- After conversion to equity → dividend paid in INR (currency risk lies with investor).

---

#### 2.2.7.5 Equity Linked Debentures (ELDs)

**Definition:**
- Floating rate debt instruments whose interest is based on returns of underlying equity asset (Nifty, Sensex, individual shares, or custom basket).

**Structure:**
- Part of principal → invested in fixed income securities (principal protection)
- Balance → used to buy options (exposure to equity returns)

**Important Cautions:**
- "Capital protection" does NOT mean absence of credit risk (risk of default by issuer).
- Credit rated by rating agencies.
- If issuer uses proceeds for CAPEX, very little left after capital protection + options.
- Better to engage an Investment Banker/Arranger to create this structured product.

---

#### 2.2.7.6 Commodity Linked Debentures (CLDs)

**Definition:**
- Floating rate debt instruments whose interest is based on returns of underlying commodity asset.
- Most common: linked to precious metals (Gold, Silver).

**Advantage to Investor:**
- Opportunity to earn return from commodity markets.
- Initial capital protected.

---

#### 2.2.7.7 Mortgage Backed Securities (MBS) and Asset Backed Securities (ABS)

**Definition:**
- Debt instruments issued by institutions against receivables and cash flows from financial assets.

**Examples:**
| Type | Underlying Asset |
|------|------------------|
| MBS | Home loans |
| ABS | Auto loans, rent receivables, credit card receivables |

**Mechanism:**
- Cash flows from these assets → meet interest and principal repayment obligations on bonds issued.
- Securitization creates liquidity from otherwise illiquid assets.

**Features:**
- Credit rated.
- May be listed on stock exchanges.

---

#### 2.2.7.8 REITs/InvITs

**Real Estate Investment Trusts (REITs):**
- Pool money from investors.
- Invest in revenue-generating real estate projects.
- Formed as a trust.
- **Requirement**: 80% of assets held in real estate assets.
- **Distribution**: At least 90% of distributable surplus cash flow to unit holders.

**Infrastructure Investment Trusts (InvITs):**
- Pool money from investors.
- Invest in revenue-generating infrastructure projects.
- Formed as a trust.
- **Requirement**: 90% of unit capital invested in revenue-generating infrastructure projects (directly or through SPV).
- **Distribution**: At least 90% of distributable surplus cash flow to unit holders.

**Common Features:**
- Enjoy favourable tax treatment if regulatory requirements met.
- Issue units to investors to raise money.

---

### 2.2.8 Commodities

**Definition:**
- Basic materials or goods that are largely **homogenous** in nature.
- Interchangeable with other goods of the same type.

**Example to distinguish:**
- Bar of gold = commodity (investor indifferent between bars of same quantity/quality)
- Gold jewellery = NOT a commodity (buyer may prefer one design over another)

**Types:**
| Type | Meaning | Examples |
|------|---------|----------|
| **Hard commodities** | Natural resources, mined or extracted | Metals, crude oil |
| **Soft commodities** | Grown (agricultural) | Grains, pulses |

**Investment Consideration:**
- Commodities and inflation are directly related → investing can help protect real value.
- Most commodities involve huge storage cost → not suitable investments.
- Certain avenues available to invest.

---

#### 2.2.8.1 Precious metals

**Examples:** Gold, Silver

**Why they are viable investments:**
- Help preserve real value of money.
- Very long life (unlike many other commodities).
- Storage cost very small compared to value.

---

#### 2.2.8.2 Commodity ETFs

**Definition:**
- Exchange Traded Fund that invests the pooled investment in a range of physical commodities.

**How to invest:**
- Buy units of the fund.
- Unit value moves with NAV of fund → moves with commodity prices.

**Advantage:**
- Storage handled by the fund → investor has no storage obligation.

**Most common:**
- Gold ETFs (easiest to store and manage).

---

#### 2.2.8.3 Managed futures contract

**Definition:**
- Portfolio of futures contracts that are actively managed by professionals.
- Instead of buying actual underlying asset, investment managers take position in futures contracts.

**How investor gains:**
- Buyer gains if price increases in future (and vice versa).
- Can gain from price rise without buying the product.

**Use:**
- Investors can invest in commodities through managed commodity futures contracts.

---

#### 2.2.8.4 Warehouse receipts

**Definition:**
- Document that shows proof of ownership of goods stored in a warehouse.

**Key feature:**
- Most are **negotiable** → title to underlying goods can be transferred by simply transferring the receipt.

## 2.3 Structure of Securities Market

**Securities Market =** Primary Market + Secondary Market (interdependent and inseparable).

| Market | Function |
|--------|----------|
| **Primary Market** (New Issue Market) | Issuers raise capital by issuing securities to investors. Fresh securities issued. |
| **Secondary Market** | Facilitates trades in already-issued securities. Enables exit for investors or entry for new investors. |

---

### 2.3.1 Primary Market (Detailed)

**Used by companies (issuers) to raise fresh capital.**

#### Types of Offerings:

| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| **Public issue** | Securities issued to members of the public; anyone eligible can participate. Retail issue. |
| **IPO (Initial Public Offer)** | First sale of corporate common shares to public. Purpose: raise equity capital for growth. SEBI eligibility criteria apply. |
| **FPO (Follow on Public Offer)** | Already listed company makes fresh issue or offer for sale to public. |
| **Private Placement** | Issuing large quantity of shares to a select set of investors (max 50 as per Companies Act 2013). |
| **Rights Issue** | Securities offered to existing shareholders as on cut-off date. They can buy at specific price. |
| **Bonus Issue** | Additional shares to existing shareholders without any consideration. From retained earnings. |
| **Onshore Offering** | Capital raised from domestic market. |
| **Offshore Offering** | Capital raised from investors outside the country. |
| **OFS (Offer for Sale)** | Existing shareholders (promoters/large investors) offer portion of their holding to public. No fresh issuance. Proceeds go to offerors. |
| **Sweat Equity** | Shares issued to employees, promoters, technocrats as reward (Sec 54, Companies Act 2013). |
| **ESOPs** | Options given to employees to buy shares at pre-determined price after vesting period. |

#### IPO Allocation (SEBI):
- At least 35% → retail investors (≤ ₹2,00,000 investment)
- Up to 50% → Qualified Institutional Investors (QIBs)
- Balance → other investors

#### Anchor Investor (introduced 2009):
- QIB who applies for ≥ ₹10 crore in a public issue (book building).
- Can be allocated up to 60% of QIB portion.
- Bidding opens one day before IPO opens to public.
- Allocation price can be lower than final issue price.
- Indicator of offer quality.

#### QIP (Qualified Institutional Placement):
- Private placement of shares by listed company to QIBs only.
- SEBI defines eligibility, quantum, pricing.

#### Preferential Issue:
- Issue of specified securities to select person/group on private placement basis.
- Excludes: public issue, rights, bonus, ESOPs, QIP, sweat equity, DRs, foreign securities.
- Must comply with SEBI pricing, disclosure, lock-in requirements + Companies Act.

---

### 2.3.2 Secondary Market (Detailed)

**Provides liquidity to instruments from primary market.**
- Dealings are between investors (issuers not involved).
- Promotes growth of primary market and capital formation.

#### Two Segments:

| Segment | Meaning |
|---------|---------|
| **OTC Market** (Over-the-Counter) | Trades directly negotiated between two or more counterparties. Securities traded and settled over the counter. |
| **Exchange Traded Market** | Trading and settlement through stock exchanges. Clearing corporation acts as counterparty and guarantees settlement. |

#### Trading:
- Formal contract to buy/sell securities.
- Indian stock exchanges: electronic order-matching system.

#### Clearing and Settlement:
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| **Clearing** | Ascertaining net obligations of buyers and sellers for a specific time period. |
| **Settlement** | Delivering shares (by seller) and paying money (by buyer). |

- OTC transactions: settled directly between counterparties.
- Exchange trades: settled through Clearing Corporation.
- **Full novation**: Clearing corporation acts as buyer to every seller and seller to every buyer → substantially reduces counterparty risk.

#### Risk Management (Exchange Traded):
Clearing corporation charges various margins:
| Margin Type | Meaning |
|-------------|---------|
| **Initial/Upfront Margin** | Percentage of transaction value based on Value At Risk (VaR) |
| **Peak Margin** | Additional intraday margin |
| **Mark-to-Market (MTM) Margin** | Notional loss suffered by outstanding trade due to price movements |

## 2.4 Various Market Participants and Their Activities

### 2.4.1 Market Intermediaries

| Intermediary | Role |
|--------------|------|
| **Stock Exchanges** | Provide trading platform (NSE, BSE, MSEI). Electronic anonymous order matching. Appoint clearing agencies and clearing banks. |
| **Depositories** | Hold securities in electronic form (NSDL, CDSL). Provide services related to demat transactions. |
| **Depository Participant (DP)** | Agent of depository. Interfaces with investors. Holds investor-level accounts. |
| **Trading Members (Stock Brokers)** | Registered members of stock exchange. Facilitate buy/sell transactions. |
| **Authorised Person (AP)** | Appointed by stockbroker as agent to reach investors (replaced sub-brokers from April 2019). |
| **Custodians** | Hold funds/securities of large clients (banks, insurance, FPI). Settle transactions, track corporate actions. |
| **Clearing Corporation** | Legal counterparty to all trades. Guarantees settlement. |
| **Clearing Banks** | Maintain accounts for clearing members. Pay-in and pay-out of funds. |
| **Merchant Bankers** | Issue managers, investment bankers, lead managers. Manage new issue process. |
| **Underwriters** | Subscribe to any portion of public offer not bought by investors. Hard vs soft underwriting. |
| **Farmer Producer Organizations (FPO)** | Collective of farmers to improve bargaining power, market access, economic wellbeing. |

---

### 2.4.2 Institutional Participants

| Participant | Role/Definition |
|-------------|----------------|
| **FPIs (Foreign Portfolio Investors)** | Entities outside India registered with SEBI to invest in India. |
| **P-Note Participants** | Instruments issued by SEBI-registered FPIs to overseas investors not registered with SEBI. |
| **Mutual Funds** | Professionally managed collective investment scheme. Pools money to purchase securities. |
| **Insurance Companies** | Life and general insurance. Major investors in equity, G-sec, bonds. |
| **Pension Funds** | Pool of retirement funds. Provide stable long-term growth. |
| **Venture Capital Funds** | Invest in early-stage enterprises with long-term growth potential. Bring managerial/technical expertise. |
| **Private Equity Firms** | Funding for growth, expansion, or buy-outs (includes venture capital). |
| **Hedge Funds** | Wide mandate across assets, products, geographies. Not necessarily hedged. |
| **AIFs (Alternative Investment Funds)** | Category I (start-ups, SMEs, infra), Category II (real estate, PE, no leverage), Category III (hedge funds, use leverage). |
| **Investment Advisers** | Help investors with asset allocation, financial planning, goal setting. |
| **Warehouse Service Providers** | Storage for commodities. Issue negotiable warehouse receipts (NWRs). Registered with WDRA. |
| **Quality Assayers** | Evaluate and certify quality of commodities. Required for NWRs. |
| **EPF (Employee Provident Fund)** | Defined benefit scheme. Mandatory contributions (12% employer + 12% employee). |
| **NPS (National Pension Scheme)** | Government-sponsored retirement scheme. Subscriber chooses funds. |
| **Family Offices** | Handle wealth, investments, estate planning, tax planning for wealthy families. |
| **Corporate Treasuries** | Temporarily invest surplus funds of companies. |

### 2.4.3 Retail Participants

- Individual investors buying/selling for personal account (not for another organization).

- **UHNI** = Ultra High Net-worth Individuals

### 2.4.4 Corporates

- Business margins affected by commodity price variations.
- Procure raw materials → convert to value-added products.

---

### 2.4.5 Proxy Advisory services firms

- Advise investors on exercise of their rights in the company.
- Recommendations on public offer or voting on agenda items.
- Analyze voting proposals and how they affect investor interest.
- Typically engaged by institutional investors.

## 2.5 Kinds of Transactions

### 2.5.1 Cash, Tom and Spot Trades/Transactions

| Type | Settlement | Meaning |
|------|------------|---------|
| **Cash Trades** | T+0 | Same trading day |
| **Tom Trades** | T+1 | Next trading day |

---

### 2.5.2 Forward transactions

- Contractual agreements between two parties.
- Buy/sell underlying asset at a future date at a price decided today.
- Both parties obliged to honour.
- OTC contracts (customized terms).
- Carry counterparty risk.

**Example:** Farmer agrees to sell wheat to miller 6 months later at a price agreed today.

---

### 2.5.3 Futures

- Standardized exchange-traded forward contracts.
- Standardized: market lots, quality, delivery terms, settlement method.
- Traded and settled on stock exchange.
- Clearing corporation provides settlement guarantee.
- Subject to stringent margin requirements.
- Available on: equities, equity indices, commodities, currencies, interest rates.

**Example (MCX Wheat Futures):**
- Trading unit: 10MT
- Delivery: physical only
- Delivery centre: Exchange approved warehouses

---

### 2.5.4 Options

**Definition:**
- Contract that gives the **right, but not the obligation**, to buy or sell underlying asset on or before a stated date at a stated price.

**Parties:**
| Party | Action |
|-------|--------|
| Buyer/Holder | Pays premium, buys the right |
| Writer/Seller | Receives premium, has obligation to sell/buy if buyer exercises |

**Types:**
| Type | Right |
|------|-------|
| **Call Option** | Right to BUY underlying at strike price on or before given date |
| **Put Option** | Right to SELL underlying at strike price on or before given date |

**Can be transacted:** OTC Market OR Exchange Traded Markets.

**Example:** Arvind buys Nifty 50 call option at 11000, 3 months, pays ₹100 premium.
- If Nifty at 11200 on settlement: In the money → exercise → gain = (200 - 100) = ₹100 net.
- If Nifty at 10800: Out of money → expires worthless → Arvind loses ₹100 premium.

---

### 2.5.5 Swaps

**Definition:**
- Derivative contract between two parties to exchange cash flows in the future according to pre-arranged formula.
- Helps manage risks: interest rates, currency rates, commodity prices.

**Example (Interest Rate Swap):**
- Borrower pays floating rate (T-bill rate + spread) on borrowing.
- Borrower prefers fixed rate.
- Enters swap: Pay fixed to swap dealer, receive T-bill + spread from swap dealer.
- Net obligation = fixed rate to swap dealer.

---

### 2.5.6 Trading, Speculating, Hedging, Arbitrage, Pledging of Shares

| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| **Trading** | Short-term (hours/days/weeks). Based on price patterns from technical analysis. Assumes historical patterns repeat. Higher probability of gains than speculators. |
| **Speculating** | Purchase/sale expecting gain from price changes. Based on private/insider information or own view on fundamentals. Usually short-term. Leveraged (magnifies gains/losses). |
| **Hedging** | Buying asset/contract to offset potential losses from existing investment. Expected result: no gain/no loss. Goal: avoid losses, not make profits. Uses forwards/futures, swaps, options. |
| **Arbitrage** | Simultaneous purchase and sale in two different markets to profit from price discrepancies. Exists only briefly in efficient markets. |
| **Pledging of Shares** | Taking loan against securities. Pledgor = investor. Pledgee = lender. Securities remain in demat account but blocked. Can be unpledged when loan repaid. Default → pledgee can sell shares after notice. |

---

## 2.6 Dematerialization and Rematerialization of securities

| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| **Dematerialization** | Converting physical securities into book entry (electronic) form. No distinctive numbers, folio numbers, certificate numbers. SEBI requires public issue companies to offer demat option. |
| **Rematerialization** | Converting electronic securities back into physical form. Securities allotted with distinctive numbers. |
