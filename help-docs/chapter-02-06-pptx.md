# 2.6 Dematerialization and Rematerialization

## Slide 1: Dematerialization: Moving to a Digital Format
*   **Definition:** The process of converting physical security certificates into electronic book-entry form.
*   **Fungibility:** Shares in demat form are interchangeable; they do not have distinctive numbers, folio numbers, or certificate numbers.
*   **Mandatory Requirement:** SEBI requires companies making a public issue to partner with all depositories to offer this electronic option.
*   **Operational Efficiency:** Eliminates the risks of physical loss, theft, or forgery of paper certificates.

### Notes
Dematerialization, or "demat," is the modern standard for holding securities in India. It is essentially the process of turning paper certificates into digital data. In this electronic form, shares become "fungible," meaning one share is exactly the same as another, similar to how one 100-rupee note is equal in value to any other. There are no unique serial numbers to track for individual shares anymore. To ensure a smooth digital ecosystem, SEBI mandates that any company launching a public issue must have agreements with depositories like NSDL and CDSL so that investors can hold their investments safely in a digital vault.

***

## Slide 2: Rematerialization: Returning to Paper
*   **Definition:** The reverse process of dematerialization, where electronic holdings are converted back into physical certificates.
*   **Process:** On an investor's request, the depository cancels the electronic entry and the company issues physical certificates.
*   **Identification:** Unlike demat shares, rematerialized shares are allotted distinctive numbers again.
*   **Usage:** This is rarely used today, as physical shares are harder to trade and require more administrative effort.

### Notes
Rematerialization is simply the reverse of the demat process. If an investor specifically wants a physical piece of paper representing their ownership, they can request to "rematerialize" their holdings. The depository will then remove the shares from their electronic account, and the company will issue new physical certificates. These paper certificates will once again have distinctive numbers and folio details. While this option exists, it is quite uncommon now because digital shares are much faster and safer to trade on modern stock exchanges.
