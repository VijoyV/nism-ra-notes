    Study Plan

    Preparation Strategy

    To help you study in parallel, I’ve refined the tracks into thematic pillars. This allows you to work through different "mindsets"—Regulatory, Internal Analysis, and External Trends—simultaneously without feeling repetitive.

    Parallel Study Tracks (100 Marks Total)

    Track 1: Market Mechanics & Regulation (35 Marks)

    Focus: How the market is built, governed, and analyzed via price action.

    • Ch 1: Intro to RA Profession (1)
    • Ch 2: Intro to Securities Market (2)
    • Ch 3: Terminology in Equity and Debt (2)
    • Ch 4: Fundamentals of Research (5)
    • Ch 14: Legal and Regulatory Environment (10)
    • Ch 15: Technical Analysis (15)

    Track 2: The Company Pillar (35 Marks)

    Focus: Deep-diving into the business itself and determining what it is worth.

    • Ch 7: Company Analysis – Business and Governance (6)
    • Ch 8: Company Analysis – Financial Analysis (12)
    • Ch 10: Valuation Principles (12)
    • Ch 13: Qualities of a Good Research Report (5)

    Track 3: Macro & Risk Pillar (30 Marks)

    Focus: Broad economic drivers and the math of managing risk.

    • Ch 5: Economic Analysis (5)
    • Ch 6: Industry Analysis (8)
    • Ch 9: Corporate Actions (5)
    • Ch 11: Fundamental Analysis of Commodities (5)
    • Ch 12: Fundamentals of Risk and Return (7)

    Logic of Parallel Learning

    These tracks are designed so that you can switch between them to avoid fatigue. Track 1 is largely rules-based, Track 2 is math/accounting-heavy, and Track 3 is conceptual/macro-focused.

    Interdependencies

    While they are parallel, there are three critical "bridge" chapters you should handle first:

    1. Chapter 3 (Terminology): You must understand the basic terms here before you can effectively analyze financials in Track 2 or economics in Track 3.

    2. Chapter 4 (Research Fundamentals): This introduces the "Top-Down" vs. "Bottom-Up" approaches, which are the primary connection points between Track 2 (Company) and Track 3 (Macro/Industry).

    3. Chapter 8 (Financials) \(\rightarrow\) Chapter 10 (Valuation): In Track 2, you cannot do Valuation effectively without first mastering the Financial Analysis in Chapter 8.

    Weeky Plan

    This 3-week parallel study plan is logically arranged to build foundational vocabulary first, followed by the "Top-Down" analytical framework, and ending with complex valuation and regulatory requirements.

    The logic uses Track 1 for rules and mechanics, Track 2 for internal business health, and Track 3 for external macro influences.

    3-Week Parallel Study Plan

    Week Track Chapter Hours
    1 1: Market Mechanics Ch 1: Intro to RA Profession & Ch 2: Securities Market 1.0
    1 1: Market Mechanics Ch 3: Terminology (Equity & Debt) 1.0
    1 2: The Company Pillar Ch 7: Company Analysis - Business and Governance 1.5
    1 3: Macro & Risk Ch 4: Fundamentals of Research 1.0
    1 3: Macro & Risk Ch 5: Economic Analysis 1.0
    2 1: Market Mechanics Ch 15: Technical Analysis 2.0
    2 2: The Company Pillar Ch 8: Company Analysis - Financial Analysis 2.0
    2 3: Macro & Risk Ch 6: Industry Analysis 1.5
    2 3: Macro & Risk Ch 9: Corporate Actions 0.5
    3 1: Market Mechanics Ch 14: Legal and Regulatory Environment 1.5
    3 2: The Company Pillar Ch 10: Valuation Principles 2.0
    3 2: The Company Pillar Ch 13: Qualities of a Good Research Report 0.5
    3 3: Macro & Risk Ch 11: Fundamental Analysis of Commodities 1.0
    3 3: Macro & Risk Ch 12: Fundamentals of Risk and Return 1.0

    Logical Arrangement & Interdependencies

    • Week 1 (The Foundation): Focuses on basic terminology (Ch 3) and the Top-Down research logic (Ch 4) before diving into deep math.
    • Week 2 (Data & Trends): Pairs the intensive study of financial statements (Ch 8) with price-action analysis (Ch 15). Understanding industry drivers (Ch 6) helps contextualize the company's financial performance.
    • Week 3 (Advanced Synthesis): Valuation (Ch 10) can only be mastered after Week 2’s financial analysis. This week concludes with the regulatory "Code of Conduct" (Ch 14) and the final output of an analyst: the research report (Ch 13).
