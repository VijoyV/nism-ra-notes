    Chapter 3: Terminology in Equity and Debt Markets

    This chapter introduces the essential "vocabulary" of finance. While it carries a weightage of 2 marks, these terms are prerequisites for understanding later chapters on financial analysis and valuation.

    3.1 Terminology in Equity Market

    Equity represents ownership and carries higher risks and potentially higher rewards compared to debt.

    I. Values and Prices

    • Face Value (FV): The nominal price of a share. It is used to calculate dividends (e.g., a 30% dividend on a ₹10 FV share is ₹3).

    • Book Value (BV): The "accounting value" or net-worth per share. It is the theoretical amount each share would get if the company wound up today.

    • Market Value: The current price at which a share trades in the stock market.

    • Intrinsic Value: The estimated "true" value of a share based on future earning potential. Investors buy when Intrinsic Value > Market Value (undervalued).

    • Replacement Value: The cost to set up an identical company with similar infrastructure today.

    II. Valuation Metrics and Ratios

    These metrics help analysts determine if a stock is cheap or expensive relative to its peers or history.

    Metric Formula Significance
    Market Cap \(\text{CMP} \times \text{Outstanding Shares}\) The total value the market places on the company.
    Enterprise Value (EV) \((\text{MV of Equity} + \text{Debt} + \text{Pref. Cap}) - \text{Cash}\) The theoretical cost to buy the entire business.
    EPS \(\text{Net Profit} / \text{Outstanding Shares}\) The amount of profit earned for every share issued.
    P/E Ratio \(\text{CMP} / \text{EPS}\) Measures what the market pays for ₹1 of earnings.
    P/S Ratio \(\text{CMP} / \text{Annual Net Sales per Share}\) Useful for valuing companies with temporary losses.
    P/BV Ratio \(\text{CMP} / \text{Book Value per Share}\) Common in banking/finance where assets are monetary.

    3.2 Terminology in Debt Market

    Debt represents a loan to an issuer. The primary risks are Interest Rate Risk (price volatility) and Credit Risk (default risk).

    I. Key Definitions

    • Coupon Rate: The fixed interest rate paid as a percentage of the Face Value.
    • Yield to Maturity (YTM): The total return expected if the bond is held until maturity, equating future cash flows to the current market price.
    • Duration: A measure of a bond's price sensitivity to interest rate changes. Rule: If interest rates rise, bond prices fall; bonds with higher duration fall more.

    II. Yield Comparisons

    Type of Yield Calculation Basis Shortcoming
    Current Yield \(\text{Coupon} / \text{Market Price}\) Ignores future cash flows and time value.
    YTM Internal Rate of Return (IRR) Assumes all coupons are reinvested at the same rate.
    Realised Yield Actual holding period return Requires knowing the actual reinvestment rate.

    3.3 Types of Bonds and Their Features

    Companies and governments modify bond features to attract different types of investors.

    Bond Type Key Characteristic
    Zero-Coupon Issued at a discount and redeemed at par; pays no periodic interest.
    Floating-Rate Coupon is reset periodically based on a benchmark (e.g., inflation).
    Convertible Can be converted into equity shares at a later date, potentially diluting EPS.
    Inflation-Protected Both principal and interest are adjusted for inflation (e.g., WPI or CPI).
    Masala Bonds Rupee-denominated bonds issued by Indian entities in foreign markets.
    Perpetual (AT1) No fixed maturity; coupons are paid only from distributable profits.

    3.4 Terminology in Commodity Markets

    • Spot Price: The current market price for immediate delivery.
    • Basis: The difference between the spot price and the futures price (\(\text{Basis} = \text{Spot} - \text{Futures}\)).
    • Contango: A situation where futures prices are higher than spot prices.
    • Backwardation: A situation where futures prices are lower than spot prices.
    • Cost of Carry: The cost of storing, insuring, and financing a physical commodity until delivery.

    Quick Facts for Revision

    • Inverse Relationship: Market interest rates and bond prices move in opposite directions.
    • Agency Risk Reduction: ESOPs and Sweat Equity align management interests with shareholders.
    • DVRs: Shares with Differential Voting Rights usually trade at a discount to common shares.
    • TTM: "Trailing Twelve Months" refers to the immediate previous 12 months used for current earnings analysis.
    • Capital Protection: PPNs (Principal-Protected Notes) aim to protect the initial investment but are still subject to the credit risk of the issuer.
