    2.1 Introduction to Security Market

    What are Securities?

    Securities are tradable financial instruments that represent either:

    • Ownership in an entity (e.g., equity shares), or
    • A debt obligation owed by the issuer to the investor (e.g., bonds, debentures).

    They are issued by companies, financial institutions, and governments to raise funds, and are purchased by investors seeking returns on their savings. Securities are also commonly referred to as financial instruments or financial products.

    Why are Securities Important?

    Securities serve the interests of both issuers and investors:

    For Issuers

    • Provide access to capital for business expansion, operations, and development.
    • Enable long-term fund raising from a large pool of investors.

    For Investors

    • Convert savings into financial assets capable of generating returns.
    • Offer opportunities for wealth creation and income generation.

    Thus, securities create a mutually beneficial relationship between those who need funds and those who have surplus funds to invest.

    Role of the Secondary Market

    One of the major advantages of securities is that investors can transfer their ownership to others through the secondary market without affecting the issuer.

    This provides:

    • Liquidity to investors (easy entry and exit)
    • Long-term capital to issuers
    • Flexibility for investors to sell investments whenever needed

    As a result, even long-term securities become effectively liquid and tradable investments.

    What is a Securities (OR Financial) Market?

    A securities market is an organized platform where buyers and sellers trade securities.

    Its primary functions are:

    • Facilitating buying and selling of securities
    • Creating liquidity
    • Enabling efficient movement of capital
    • Converting savings into investments

    By bringing together a large number of buyers and sellers, the market helps securities trade at prices close to their fair market value.

    Economic Importance of Securities Markets

    Securities markets play a critical role in the economy by:

    • Mobilizing idle savings
    • Channeling funds into productive activities
    • Supporting business growth and economic development
    • Providing investors with a wide range of investment opportunities

    In simple terms, securities markets act as a bridge between savers and borrowers.

    Components of the Indian Financial Market

    Four Major Participants

    Participant Role Examples
    Investors / Providers of Funds Supply capital by purchasing securities Retail Investors, HNIs, Mutual Funds, Insurance Companies, Pension Funds, FPIs, AIFs
    Borrowers / Seekers of Funds Raise capital by issuing securities Companies, Startups, PSUs, Government of India, State Governments, Banks, Financial Institutions
    Intermediaries Facilitate issuance, trading, settlement and custody of securities NSE, BSE, MCX, NCDEX, Stock Brokers, NSDL, CDSL, Depository Participants, Clearing Corporations, Merchant Bankers, RTAs, Credit Rating Agencies, AMCs
    Regulatory Bodies Ensure fair, transparent and orderly market functioning SEBI, RBI, Ministry of Finance, IRDAI, PFRDA, IFSCA, IBBI

    Intermediaries in the Indian Financial Market

    Intermediary Function Examples
    Stock Exchanges Provide platform for buying and selling securities NSE, BSE, MCX, NCDEX
    Stock Brokers Execute trades on behalf of investors Zerodha, Angel One, ICICI Direct, Groww, Motilal Oswal
    Depositories Hold securities in electronic form NSDL, CDSL
    Depository Participants (DPs) Act as agents of depositories and provide demat accounts HDFC Securities, SBI Securities, ICICI Securities, Zerodha
    Clearing Corporations Ensure settlement of trades and manage counterparty risk NSE Clearing Ltd., ICCL, MCXCCL
    Merchant Bankers / Investment Bankers Manage IPOs, FPOs, rights issues and capital raising ICICI Securities, Axis Capital, Kotak Mahindra Capital, SBI Capital Markets
    Registrars & Transfer Agents (RTAs) Maintain investor records and process corporate actions KFin Technologies, MUFG Intime India (formerly Link Intime), Cameo Corporate Services
    Credit Rating Agencies (CRAs) Assess creditworthiness of debt issuers and instruments CRISIL, ICRA, CARE Ratings, India Ratings, Acuité Ratings
    Asset Management Companies (AMCs) Manage mutual fund schemes and investor funds SBI MF, HDFC MF, ICICI Prudential MF, Nippon India MF

    Definition of Securities under SCRA, 1956

    Section 2(h) of the Securities Contracts (Regulation) Act, 1956 (SCRA) defines securities broadly to include:

    Traditional Securities

    • Shares
    • Stocks
    • Bonds
    • Debentures
    • Debenture stock

    Investment and Market-linked Instruments

    • Derivatives
    • Mutual Fund Units
    • Collective Investment Scheme Units
    • Pooled Investment Vehicle Units

    Specialized Instruments

    • Security Receipts
    • Securitized Debt Instruments
    • Government Securities
    • Rights and Interests in Securities
    • Electronic Gold Receipts (declared securities in 2021)

    Exclusion

    • Unit Linked Insurance Policies (ULIPs) are specifically excluded from the definition of securities.

    Broad Categories of Securities

    Investors in the Indian securities market can choose from three broad categories of securities:

    1. Equity Securities

    Represent ownership in a company.

    Examples:

    • Equity Shares
    • Preference Shares

    2. Debt Securities

    Represent borrowing by the issuer.

    Examples:

    • Bonds
    • Debentures
    • Government Securities

    3. Derivative Securities

    Derive their value from an underlying asset.

    Examples:

    • Futures
    • Options

    The choice among these categories depends primarily on an investor's risk appetite, return expectations, and investment objectives.

    Key Takeaway

    The securities market is a vital mechanism that channels savings into productive investments. It allows issuers to raise funds efficiently while providing investors with opportunities to earn returns, maintain liquidity, and participate in economic growth. Securities can broadly be classified into equity, debt, and derivatives, each catering to different investment needs and risk profiles.
